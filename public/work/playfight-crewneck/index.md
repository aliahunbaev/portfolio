Frank Lifshitz dreamed of being an artist. But when he immigrated to America with his wife, he ended up working long hours as a house painter to support his kids.

He didn't give up. He changed their name to Lauren, thinking "Frank Lauren" would be taken more seriously as an artist than "Frank Lifshitz." Sadly, he never saw his dreams come true — but his son did.

Ralph Lauren took their creative and dirt-poor background and built one of the most iconic American fashion labels, out of nothing but taste and ambition.

Through Polo, he was able to create — and live out — the gorgeous world of old money he dreamed of as a child.

![Young Ralph pictured in New York. He was known for having the wildest fits in primary school.](/images/journal/playfight/ralph-lauren-young.jpg)

I'd written Polo off as country-club fashion, but after reading his biography, I found myself caught in its charm.

Especially in the knit sweaters — these beautiful storybook images painted in yarn. They felt warm in a way most fashion doesn't. Like they could've been made by your grandmother. A garment of love that tells a story and lasts a lifetime.

![](/images/journal/playfight/polo-knit.jpg)

I wanted to use the medium of a knit sweater to capture the joy of being a kid and playfighting. Wrestling with your cousins. Fucking around with your homies in class. Getting in trouble and laughing it off.

That kind of loving conflict disappears too early in life. Adults tend to avoid tension or weaponize it. But when we were kids, even fighting had joy in it. I think we need some of that lightness back in our days.

![](/images/journal/playfight/kids-boxing.jpg)

Before making movies, Stanley Kubrick was running around New York as a photographer for Look Magazine.

When he was 18, he shot a beautiful picture of a kids' boxing club. That photo became my reference for the illustration.

![](/images/journal/playfight/kubrick-and-sketches.jpg)

![](/images/journal/playfight/final-playfight-knit.jpg)

I like taking on projects I've never done before. It forces you to learn. To make the knit sweater, I hopped on knitting forums on Reddit.

That's where I met Marilyn, a 68-year-old lady who FaceTimed me and explained how to turn my drawing into a knit pattern. She told me to say hi to you.

I'm glad she helped, because I wanted the Playfight Crewneck to feel like it was passed down by someone who knew and took care of you as a child.

Someone who remembers you as a little kid, running around, fucking shit up, with nothing on your mind but joy — and who reminds you that you still get to be that person sometimes.

You don't grow out of childhood joy. You grow away from it.

But it's still there, waiting.
